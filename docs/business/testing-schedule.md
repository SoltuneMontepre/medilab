# Testing schedule

- Sales owns the committed return date it promises the customer. A sample test's internal due date is that date minus the safety margin, 2 days by default, at the end of the company-local day, and is met when the result is submitted. A margin change does not rewrite stored dates. The Head of Department may override an internal date with a reason; a changed committed date recalculates only automatic dates and reports conflicts with overrides.
- A booking schedules one sample test for one authorized tester and one interval, on a machine only when the way needs one, with every bookable location its steps name. The machine-and-method link gives the duration, which covers preparation, measurement and time to repeat a failed measurement, and a capacity in samples, 1 by default. Bookings whose ways use the same method may share a machine for exactly the same interval up to that capacity; nothing joins a machine once its interval has started. The system does not count or limit measurement attempts.
- A sample test can be booked only once its sample is received by its department, is not held for the customer's agreement and is not cancelled. The Lab Head maintains person-to-parameter authorization without validity dates. Testing tasks queue by department. The head assigns authorized people or they claim the work; team queues are not used.
- Each department has a proposed plan that places every received, not-started sample test one at a time in priority order, in the earliest interval where every required resource is free. A proposed slot reserves nothing; accepting it reserves its tester, machine and locations together, or nothing. Priority classes are urgent, due soon within the configured threshold of 24 hours, and normal; within a class the earlier committed date goes first, then the earlier arrival.
- Check the tester, required locations and machine when needed against the company calendar, holidays, shifts, approved leave and unavailable intervals. A public holiday the administrator marked as a working day is accepted. A machine booking needs valid calibration and cannot end after it; planned maintenance reserves downtime and overdue maintenance blocks new bookings until completion. Returning a machine to in use after a repair requires a calibration or maintenance record dated after the repair.
- The feasibility check only reads: it simulates in-house resources and outsourced turnaround, including transport and processing, explains infeasibility and gives the earliest feasible date. Missing outsourced turnaround makes the estimate incomplete. A date the head keeps despite infeasibility stays flagged at risk and implies no reserved resources.
- A recalculation that moves scheduled bookings is a plan revision, approved by the head of each affected department for their moves. A move whose planned finish passes its internal due date would miss a committed date: it is held back, and its booking keeps its slot, until Sales records the customer's agreement to a new date. A booking the head places or moves by hand is pinned and is never moved by a revision.
- The head records planned OverTime for a tester, an outside-hours interval and a reason; the scheduler never creates, moves or recalculates it, and it extends only the tester's availability. Record only planned OverTime, not actual hours or payroll.
- A booking holds its machine until the result is entered or the measurement is marked finished. A booking that overruns keeps the machine, flags the next bookings on it at risk and notifies its tester and head.
- Approved leave or required-location unavailability returns overlapping not-started bookings to the plan and notifies the head. A machine breakdown or lost calibration during a booking ends it and keeps the recorded data as an interrupted result, which the head assesses as usable, needing a retest or needing more sample. A retest is a new result with a new booking, on another machine only when it is linked to the same method. Cancelling a sample test ends only its own booking.
- No resource failure silently changes the accepted way. An alternative method or subcontractor requires Sales to obtain customer agreement through a revised quotation. While Sales waits for that agreement, the sample test is flagged at risk and keeps its accepted way; nothing is booked on an unapproved way or subcontractor. A changed committed date needs the customer's agreement, recording old date, new date and confirmation. Refusal leads to a cancellation request for the unfulfillable part, not automatic cancellation.
- A sample test is at risk when its planned finish is after its internal due date, when no free slot lets it finish by then, or when its booking overran. Notify its tester and head initially and in daily consolidated summaries until resolved. Only a risk to the committed date starts the Sales workflow; internal-only due-date changes do not notify customers.
- The calibration, maintenance and recent-repair warning windows are three independent settings, each 7 days by default. They never override a booking block. Collection appointments include a configurable 60-minute travel buffer. A moved booking replaces unsent reminders and notifies immediately if already inside the reminder window, once per update.
- Work waiting for someone is a task. Tasks use their shared lifecycle, while the business state belongs to the document. A tester's testing task completes when they submit the result; approving it is the head's work at the result approval level. A rejected result keeps its record and review, and opens a new testing task for the same sample test.
