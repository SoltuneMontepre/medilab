# Testing schedule

- Sales owns the customer's promised return date. An internal sample-test due date defaults to the end of the company-local day, 2 safety-margin days before it. A margin change does not rewrite stored dates. The Head of Department may override an internal date with a reason; a changed customer date recalculates only automatic dates and warns about conflicts with manual overrides.
- A run uses one agreed way of testing, one authorized tester and one interval. Its attempts may come from several samples, customers and requests within capacity. A machine is required only when the way needs one. Machine-and-way configuration gives duration and capacity; no-machine ways have their own duration and capacity, default capacity 1.
- The Lab Head maintains person-to-parameter authorization without validity dates. Testing tasks queue by department. The head assigns authorized people or they claim the work; team queues are not used.
- Check the tester, required locations and machine when needed against the company calendar, holidays, shifts, approved leave and unavailable intervals. A public holiday the administrator marked as a working day is accepted. Machine bookings need valid calibration, including for a new machine; planned maintenance reserves downtime and overdue maintenance blocks new bookings until completion.
- Before accepting a date, simulate in-house resources and outsourced turnaround, including transport and processing. The latest branch finish plus the safety margin gives the estimate. Missing outsourced turnaround prevents a complete estimate. The check explains infeasibility and the earliest feasible date, and reserves or moves nothing. A Head of Department may keep an infeasible internal date with a reason and risk flag.
- A test is urgent within the configured threshold, 24 hours by default, or when its tester or head marks it urgent with a reason. Queued work is planned urgent first, then in arrival order. Explicit bumping moves only work that has not started, and the head decides what to do about affected deadlines.
- The head may book outside normal hours with a reason without an HR overtime shift. Record only planned OverTime for the tester, work, outside-hours interval, reason and assigner, not actual hours or payroll.
- Approved leave or required-location unavailability returns overlapping not-started work to the queue and notifies its head. Machine disruption replans not-started work on another machine for the same way, or keeps it queued. Preserve interrupted-run data; the head assesses each attempt as usable, needing a retest or needing more sample. Retests create new attempts in new runs linked to the interruption.
- No resource failure silently changes the accepted way. An alternative method or subcontractor requires Sales to obtain customer agreement through a revised quotation. If the promised date is at risk, Sales informs the customer and obtains agreement before changing it, recording old date, new date and confirmation. Refusal leads to a cancellation request for the unfulfillable part, not automatic cancellation.
- A test unable to finish by its internal due date, or whose planned measurement ended without a result, is at risk. Notify its tester and head initially and in daily consolidated summaries until resolved. Internal-only due-date changes do not notify customers.
- The calibration, maintenance and recent-repair warning windows are three independent settings, each 7 days by default. They never override a booking block. Collection appointments include a configurable 60-minute travel buffer. A moved booking replaces unsent reminders and notifies immediately if already inside the reminder window, once per update.
- Work waiting for someone is a task. Tasks use their shared lifecycle, while the business state belongs to the document. A testing task completes when its reported result is approved; completing one attempt does not complete a shared run's other work.
